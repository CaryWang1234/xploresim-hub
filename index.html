Before you read those numbers
  • The baselines separate: greedy beats a random intent 16/16 MATCHes, margin 167.5 ± 8.1.
  • Every BRANCH still fills and the TROUGH does not: a CORAL has to be lined up before it is pushed and can fall back onto the carpet, which costs an alliance 14.0 ± 3.1 points a MATCH (8.0 pieces of 24 an ALLIANCE-MATCH, and the trough fills 11.2 of its 24 against 15.0 when nothing can miss).
  • The NET has a second feeder now: netScoreSec is a ROBOT property, the shipped trio carries one, and over 16 MATCHes it books 11.1 deposits a MATCH. Both fleets having it is worth −26.8 ± 4.2 points a MATCH and one side having it is worth +10.3 ± 4.0, because the rings in your NET are the ones your opponent no longer pushed through their PROCESSOR.
  • Defense is a negative-margin action here: one pressing seat takes 7.0 ± 1.7 off the opponent and costs its own ALLIANCE 5.9 ± 1.9, because contact inside a protected zone is a MAJOR for the other side whoever starts it.
  • Named presets: CORAL totals from TBA alliance accounts, split between the three ROBOTs only by Statbotics 2025 EPA ratios. That reproduces Johnson at 58.1 and Newton at 58.8.
  • Drop the PROCESSOR column and the generic three-ROBOT alliance mean is 249.4 against 260.56 over 32 playoff alliance sides.
  • Per-ROBOT tempo is drawn ±20% each MATCH. The shipped fingerprint is 65be7627, and the two off values have to hand back the builds they came from: e1eaef89 with no tempo spread, 7856316 with no placement model and aad9e965 with neither.
  • The planner searches the climb window, the ALGAE duty and the CORAL STATION queue, and pins three budget rules the baseline leaves off: a cycle needs 4 s of clock to spare, the hopper is spent before the CAGE, and a piece a teammate is bound for costs 1.82 s of drive. All three together are +11.85 ± 3.17 combined points a MATCH on held-out seeds. It beats the shipped baseline, and it only reproduces the best fixed rule: writing the answer down scores the same.

Four rules both seasons follow

  • Clause beside the constantEvery value sits next to its manual clause. Where the manual contradicts itself, the choice is a switch on the page — never a silent pick.
  • Mirror means one roster on both sidesThree identical ROBOTs each alliance, seats swapped every other MATCH. That turns a strategy comparison into a ROBOT comparison; an earlier layout faked a 60-point field asymmetry by getting this wrong.
  • Every physics knob has an off valueA new mechanism ships as an ablatable experiment arm, not a new default. Paired arms run on the same seeds; unpaired mean differences carry seed variance.
  • Quote n and the standard errorA single mirror MATCH is ±2%; a 96-MATCH arm is about ±5 win points. A win-rate change smaller than its error bar is not a result.
